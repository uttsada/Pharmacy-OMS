Serializing medicine after it's already packed

A web order management system that lets pharmacy staff give every packaged item a unique serial number, check it with a scan, and print a compliant label.

The problem

New rules require a serial number on every personalized medicine

Serial numbers are usually applied on the packaging line. Stock that was packed before the rule changed has no serial, so it cannot ship compliantly.

The company needs a way to serialize orders that are already packed: generate a serial per item, confirm each physical item with a scan, and print a label for it. Access must be controlled, because staff with different jobs need different rights.

    Users and flow

    Four roles, one hand-off from warehouse to pharmacist

    Each role sees only the menus and actions it is allowed to use. The Admin can change that matrix without a code release.

    Admin

    Manages users, roles and settings. Can view orders and the audit log.

    Pharmacist / QA lead

    Supervisor

    Creates and imports orders, reviews labeled orders and releases them for shipping.

    Warehouse packer

    Operator

    Builds the cart, generates serials, scans each item and applies its label.

    Internal or external QA

    Auditor

    Read-only access to orders, serial numbers and the audit log.

    1. SupervisorCreates or imports an order with destination and delivery date.
    2. OperatorAdds products to the cart by GTIN, batch and quantity.
    3. SystemOn Confirm, reserves one unique serial number per unit.
    4. OperatorScans each packed item. GTIN, batch and expiry are checked.
    5. SystemBinds the next serial to the item and prints its label.
    6. SupervisorReviews the fully labeled order and releases it.

    Key product decisions

    Four calls that shaped the MVP

    ERP integration waits for Release 2

    Why
    Pilot volume is small. Manual entry and CSV import get the MVP live about two weeks sooner and avoid depending on the ERP team.
    Trade-off
    Some double entry during the pilot.

    Scan first, then print

    Why
    A label prints only after the physical item passes validation, so a serial cannot land on the wrong product or an expiring pack.
    Trade-off
    One scan per item instead of printing a whole batch of labels up front.

    A reprint keeps the same serial

    Why
    One serial belongs to one physical item for life. Every reprint needs a reason and is logged.
    Trade-off
    After two reprints the operator needs a supervisor; approval arrives in Release 2.

    Random 12-character serials

    Why
    Sequential serials are easy to guess and forge. New serials are checked against every serial ever issued, including voided ones.
    Trade-off
    Serials carry no meaning on their own; lookups go through the system.

    Definition of Done

    Done means tested on real hardware and traceable

    A story is Done only when every item below is true. A release adds the checks that compliance sign-off needs.

    Every story

    • Code peer-reviewed (at least 1 approval) and merged to main
    • Unit tests written, at least 80% coverage on new code, CI green
    • All acceptance criteria pass, verified by QA on staging
    • Role permissions enforced on every new screen and API, with a negative test
    • Regulated actions (generate, print, reprint, void, release) write an audit entry
    • Tested with the real scanner and label printer at the staging station
    • UI matches the approved wireframe in the latest Chrome and Edge
    • No open critical or high bugs
    • API documentation and user guide updated
    • Accepted by the PO in the sprint review

    Every release

    • Deployed to staging through CI/CD; full regression suite passes
    • UAT signed off by the QA pharmacist
    • Scan-to-label time of 10 seconds or less at the pilot station
    • Security scan shows no high-severity findings
    • Release notes published and rollback plan tested
